2. In short

  • Nonsensor needs no account.
  • The game asks for no permission you would have to confirm: no microphone, no camera, no location, no contacts. The one exception: if you turn on the daily puzzle reminder in the settings, Android asks once whether the game may send you notifications (from Android 13). The reminder works entirely on your device and sends nothing.
  • Whatever your phone’s sensors measure (orientation, motion, light, proximity) and whatever settings the game reads (volume, brightness, dark mode, charging state, airplane mode, time) is evaluated on your device only, to solve puzzles. These values are neither stored nor transmitted.
  • Data leaves your device through the Google services built into the game: advertising, purchases and, if you sign in, Google Play Games.
  • The game itself fetches new levels and settings from a web space, and, only if you agree, sends anonymous counters. It never sends an identifier of you or your phone.

3. What is stored on your device

Your save (solved levels, sparks, settings, skins) lives in the app’s private storage on your device. It is removed when you uninstall the app or clear its data. If Android’s device backup is enabled, the save may be part of that backup in your Google account.

The game also keeps there the levels and settings it has fetched (section 7). If you take part in anonymous counting (section 8), it keeps the counters still waiting to be sent, a note of what has already been counted so that nothing is counted twice, and the variant drawn for your installation. None of this is an identifier, and it is not part of the device backup.

4. Advertising (Google AdMob)

The game shows ads through Google AdMob, a service of Google Ireland Limited, Gordon House, Barrow Street, Dublin 4, Ireland. Ads appear no earlier than after the tenth solved level. Where the law requires it, we ask for your choice through Google’s consent platform before the first ad.

To deliver ads, to measure them and to prevent fraud, Google collects through its advertising software in the app: your device’s advertising ID and other device identifiers, your IP address (from which an approximate location is derived), information about your device, how you interact with ads and with the app (for example app launches, taps and video views) and diagnostic data such as launch time and energy use. Personalised ads are shown only with your consent; without it you receive non-personalised ads. You can change your choice at any time under “Privacy options” in the game’s settings, and you can reset or delete your advertising ID in your Android settings.

Legal basis: your consent (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR) where it is required; otherwise our legitimate interest in funding a free game (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

More information: policies.google.com/technologies/ads and business.safety.google/privacy.

5. Purchases (Google Play)

Purchases in the game are handled by Google Play. We receive no payment details. The app only learns from Google which products belong to your account in order to unlock them; this information stays on your device. Legal basis: performance of the contract (Art. 6(1)(b) GDPR).

6. Google Play Games (optional)

If you are signed in to Google Play Games, the game sends unlocked achievements, leaderboard values (streak, lights, daily puzzle time) and, if you use the backup, your save to your Google account. Google also processes your Play Games player ID and gamer profile (name, avatar) and collects analytics and diagnostic data about its games software. None of this happens without signing in. You can delete this data in your Play Games profile. Legal basis: your consent by signing in (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR).

7. New levels and settings (GitHub Pages)

So that new levels and corrections reach you without an app update, the game fetches a small signed package from a web space at GitHub Pages when it starts, at most every six hours. GitHub Pages is a service of GitHub, Inc., 88 Colin P. Kelly Jr. Street, San Francisco, CA 94107, USA. The request contains no identifier of you or your phone and no cookies. As with every request on the internet, GitHub receives your IP address and may log it to keep its service secure. The game checks a digital signature before it uses anything from the package.

The package can also carry settings of the game, such as the price of a hint in sparks or the pause between two ads. Ads can never become more frequent this way than in the version you installed.

Legal basis: our legitimate interest in keeping the game up to date and fixing errors quickly (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR). More information: GitHub’s privacy statement.

8. Counting anonymously (optional)

Before your first daily puzzle the game asks once whether it may count anonymously. Only if you agree does it send counters to a database we run at Google Firebase (Firebase Realtime Database, a service of Google Ireland Limited, Gordon House, Barrow Street, Dublin 4, Ireland). The database is located in Belgium.

What is counted:

  • for each level: that it was opened for the first time, solved for the first time, solved with a paid hint, or skipped;
  • for the daily puzzle: that it was started and that it was solved, at most once a day;
  • when we compare two variants of a setting (for example two prices for a hint): how often levels were solved, hints opened, ads shown and purchases made in the variant your installation was randomly drawn into, and on how many days the game was opened there.

Each transmission says nothing but “this counter plus one”. It contains no identifier of you, your phone or your installation, no advertising ID, no time and no order of events; the database holds only sums. Which variant was drawn for your installation is stored on your phone only. Technically your IP address is transmitted, as with every request on the internet; the game neither stores it nor links it to the counters.

Withdrawing your consent: you can switch counting off at any time in the game under Settings, Privacy, “Count anonymously”. Counters still waiting on your phone are then deleted without being sent. Sums that have already been counted cannot be traced back to you and therefore cannot be removed one by one.

The solve rate: the daily puzzle shows what share of players solved it today, once at least 20 have started it. For this the game reads two numbers from the same database, at most every 30 minutes, whether or not you take part in counting. The request names only the day. Nothing about you is sent, apart from the IP address that every request carries.

Legal basis: your consent for counting (Art. 6(1)(a) GDPR); our legitimate interest in showing the solve rate (Art. 6(1)(f) GDPR).

9. Other technical processes

  • Rating: the game may open Google Play’s review dialog. The app does not learn whether or what you rate.
  • Shared puzzle cards: when you share a puzzle card, the game hands an image and a link to the app you choose. If someone installs the game through such a link, it reads once which level to open, as passed along by Google Play. This contains no personal data.
  • Reporting a problem: through “This level is broken” you can send us an e-mail. It contains the level ID, the app version, the device model, the Android version and the list of available sensors. You see the text before sending and decide yourself whether to send it. We use your message and your e-mail address only to answer you and to fix the problem, and delete them once the matter is settled.

10. Storage period, deletion and security

We store no personal data about you ourselves, apart from e-mails you send us. The web space for new levels only delivers files, and the counter database holds only sums that cannot be traced to anyone (section 8). Data processed by Google is stored according to Google’s own retention rules; you can manage it in your Google account, in your Play Games profile and in the ad settings of your device. Everything the game and Google’s software in the app transmit is encrypted in transit.

Step-by-step instructions: How to delete your data.

11. Transfers outside the EU

Google and GitHub may process data in the United States and other countries. Both rely on the EU-US Data Privacy Framework and on standard contractual clauses.

12. Children

Nonsensor is intended for people aged 13 and over and is not directed at children.

13. Your rights

You have the right of access, rectification, erasure, restriction of processing, data portability and objection, and the right to withdraw consent at any time. You can also lodge a complaint with a data protection authority. To exercise your rights, write to us at the address above.

14. Changes

When the game changes, we update this policy. The current version is always on this page.

8. Anonym mitzählen (freiwillig)

Vor deinem ersten Tagesrätsel fragt das Spiel einmal, ob es anonym mitzählen darf. Nur wenn du zustimmst, sendet es Zähler an eine Datenbank, die wir bei Google Firebase betreiben (Firebase Realtime Database, ein Dienst der Google Ireland Limited, Gordon House, Barrow Street, Dublin 4, Irland). Die Datenbank steht in Belgien.

Gezählt wird:

  • je Level: dass es zum ersten Mal geöffnet, zum ersten Mal gelöst, mit einem bezahlten Hinweis gelöst oder übersprungen wurde;
  • beim Tagesrätsel: dass es begonnen und dass es gelöst wurde, höchstens einmal am Tag;
  • wenn wir zwei Varianten eines Einstellwerts vergleichen (zum Beispiel zwei Preise für einen Hinweis): wie oft in der Variante, in die deine Installation zufällig gelost wurde, Level gelöst, Hinweise geöffnet, Anzeigen gezeigt und Käufe getätigt wurden und an wie vielen Tagen das Spiel dort geöffnet war.

Jede Übertragung sagt nichts als „dieser Zähler plus eins“. Sie enthält keine Kennung von dir, deinem Handy oder deiner Installation, keine Werbe-ID, keine Uhrzeit und keine Reihenfolge der Ereignisse; in der Datenbank stehen nur Summen. In welche Variante deine Installation gelost wurde, ist nur auf deinem Handy gespeichert. Technisch wird wie bei jedem Abruf im Internet deine IP-Adresse übertragen; das Spiel speichert sie nicht und verknüpft sie nicht mit den Zählern.

Einwilligung widerrufen: Du kannst das Mitzählen jederzeit im Spiel unter Einstellungen, Datenschutz, „Anonym mitzählen“ ausschalten. Zähler, die noch auf deinem Handy warten, werden dann gelöscht, ohne gesendet zu werden. Summen, die schon gezählt sind, lassen sich nicht auf dich zurückführen und deshalb auch nicht einzeln entfernen.

Die Lösungsquote: Das Tagesrätsel zeigt, welcher Anteil der Spieler es heute gelöst hat, sobald mindestens 20 es begonnen haben. Dafür liest das Spiel zwei Zahlen aus derselben Datenbank, höchstens alle 30 Minuten, gleich ob du mitzählst oder nicht. Der Abruf nennt nur den Tag. Über dich wird dabei nichts gesendet, abgesehen von der IP-Adresse, die jeder Abruf trägt.

Rechtsgrundlage: deine Einwilligung für das Mitzählen (Art. 6 Abs. 1 Buchst. a DSGVO); unser berechtigtes Interesse, die Lösungsquote zu zeigen (Art. 6 Abs. 1 Buchst. f DSGVO).
